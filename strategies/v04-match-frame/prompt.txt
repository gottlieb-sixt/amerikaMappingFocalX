You are matching vehicle damage across two DIFFERENT inspection systems. You get
ONE physical damage recorded in the fleet database (possibly recorded multiple
times — all its records and reference photos are shown), and a numbered list of
CANDIDATE damages that an AI inspection (FocalX) reported on the same car. For
EACH candidate independently, decide whether it is the SAME real-world damage on
the car as the database damage.

THIS IS NOT A DUPLICATE / IMAGE-SIMILARITY TASK. The two photo sets come from
different capture systems, distances and angles, so they will look quite
different even when they show the exact same damage:
- database photos: automated ENTRY-GATE SCANNER frames (low-resolution, striped
  zebra deflectometry pattern, a colored box marks the spot), or smartphone
  close-ups, or occasionally a license-plate / whole-car identification shot
  that is UNRELATED to the damage (ignore those);
- candidate photos: FocalX smartphone close-ups, often zoomed in with a
  highlight box.
Do NOT require the images to look alike. Ask only: is this the SAME physical
damage, at the SAME location on the same car, just captured differently?
Multiple photos of one damage = same damage from different angles/lighting.
Do not infer transitively across candidates.

SIDE / WHEEL / CORNER COME FROM METADATA
The database record's side/projection and each candidate's FocalX "photo
positions" label (e.g. afront-right-wheel, aleft-front, rear) are reliable for
side (left/right), corner and wheel (front/rear). Use these labels — not your
own read of the cropped image — to decide whether two damages are on the same
side/wheel/corner. Only treat them as different sides/wheels when the labels say
so.

ANNOTATION HANDLING
Photos may contain highlight boxes, arrows or overlays marking the area of
interest. Treat these as pointers only — do not read box borders, overlay edges
or creases at an annotation boundary as damage. Rulers, tape measures, a hand or
fingers near the area, and hand-drawn chalk/marker circles are inspection
artifacts, not damage and not comparison features. Their presence in one photo
set but not the other MUST NOT drive a "Different damage" verdict. Compare only
the underlying damage (location, sub-location, shape signature).

PER-DAMAGE ASSESSMENT
First extract, for the database damage and for each candidate:
- location: vehicle part + position (e.g. "front bumper, lower right")
- type: scratch | gouge | dent | crack | transfer_mark | paint_chip | tear | other
- severity: minor | moderate | severe
- shape_signature: 2-3 short phrases describing distinctive geometry
- photo_quality: usable | partial | unusable (one-line reason if not usable)

DECISION (per candidate, independently)
Criteria in order of weight:
1. Vehicle part and side/corner — same part on the same side/corner of the car
   (decide this from the metadata labels). The exact sub-position WITHIN a panel
   is NOT reliable across these differently-framed photos and must not be
   weighted heavily.
2. Shape signature — the primary discriminator when photo quality allows it.
3. Severity and approximate dimensions.
4. Damage type. Cause and colour are NOT criteria; lighting, glare and white
   balance frequently differ between the two systems.

ANGLE-INDUCED SHAPE COLLAPSE: from a low or oblique angle, multiple parallel
marks may collapse into a single diagonal line. If the sub-location is
consistent and the extent plausible, treat this as a distorting factor and
lower confidence to Medium — never "Different damage" on this effect alone.

CONFIDENCE ANCHORS
- High (Same damage): same part and same side/corner AND consistent shape
  signature; nothing contradicts.
- Medium (Same damage): same part and same side/corner and the damage is
  plausibly the same, but a distorting factor (poor light, glare, angle,
  annotation overlay, low-res scanner frame, apparent-but-unconfirmed
  sub-position difference) could explain a geometric difference. When part and
  side/corner agree and nothing clearly contradicts, prefer "Same damage" at
  Medium over "Different damage".
- Different damage (High): reserve for a CLEARLY different vehicle part, a
  different side/corner/wheel confirmed by the metadata labels, or a shape
  signature that is unambiguously incompatible in usable photos. An apparent
  sub-position difference within the same panel is NOT enough — use Medium or
  Insufficient evidence instead.
- Insufficient evidence (Low): a photo set is unusable, or the relevant part is
  not clearly visible in both sets.

OUTPUT — return ONLY valid JSON, no preamble, no markdown:
{
 "db_damage": {"location": "...", "type": "...", "severity": "...",
               "shape_signature": "...", "photo_quality": "usable"},
 "candidates": [
   {"id": 1, "location": "...", "type": "...", "severity": "...",
    "shape_signature": "...", "photo_quality": "usable",
    "verdict": "Same damage | Different damage | Insufficient evidence",
    "confidence": "Low | Medium | High",
    "reason": "<one sentence citing the decisive features>"}
 ],
 "matches": [<ids with verdict Same damage>],
 "recommendation": "auto_match | review | not_duplicate"
}
recommendation rules: auto_match = at least one Same damage at High confidence
and no Insufficient evidence; review = any Same damage only at Medium/Low OR any
Insufficient evidence; not_duplicate = every candidate is Different damage.
